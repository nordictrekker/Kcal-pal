## NOOM

The most polarizing of the three. Loved for its psychology-first philosophy; widely resented for billing practices severe enough to trigger a class-action settlement and FTC scrutiny.

### What users LOVE
- **Psychology/behavior-change approach over pure calorie counting.** "Noom's coaching and lessons address root causes of eating issues rather than just calories, particularly for those with emotional eating patterns." Succeeders "actually read and reflect on the daily lessons." (https://nutrola.app/en/blog/noom-vs-myfitnesspal-for-beginners-2026; https://healthorskin.com/is-noom-really-worth-it/)
- **Daily lessons + quizzes + accountability nudges.** "Through interactive lessons and quizzes, users learn about nutrition, exercise, and the psychology of eating." Coach check-ins and group interactions "keep beginners engaged during the critical first 30 days." (https://orionhealthwellness.com/noom-diet-review/)
- **Reported efficacy.** "Almost 78 Percent Of People Who Used This Nutrition App Said They Lost Weight." (https://www.aol.com/nutritionists-users-lot-feelings-opinions-171600324.html)

### What users HATE
- **Aggressive/deceptive billing — the dominant complaint.** "They advertise it as only $20 a month but what they don't say is they charge 8 months upfront after the trial." "Noom does not give you a notice that your subscription is coming to an end. They just charge you again at a high rate." (https://www.nutritionnc.com/glp-1/reviews/noom/)
- **Hard cancellation / dark patterns.** "Ridiculously difficult to cancel" with "multiple screens attempting to get users not to cancel that must be clicked through." "Noom is hard sell — with pages and pages of add ons and you have to decline them all or you are irrevocably charged for them." This case is catalogued as a dark-pattern legal case (https://www.deceptive.design/cases/geraldine-mahood-v-noom-inc; https://www.consumeraffairs.com/news/noom-auto-renewal-charges-lead-to-spike-in-consumer-complaints-082120.html)
- **Class-action + FTC context.** Noom agreed to a **~$62M settlement** ($56M cash + $6M subscription credits) over "risk-free" trials that "force them into automatic, costly renewals that were difficult to cancel." Noom allegedly extracted "up to eight months of nonrefundable payments totaling as much as $199" after trials expired. Settlement class: subscribers May 2016–Oct 2020; payouts ~$30–$167/person. Settlement terms require Noom to make pricing clearer, "make it easier to cancel," and improve support. (https://www.euronews.com/next/2022/02/14/noom-settlement; https://journals.law.unc.edu/ncjolt/blogs/diet-app-noom-agrees-to-pay-56-million-to-settle-class-suit/) Broader regulatory backdrop: FTC's 2024 "click-to-cancel"/Negative Option Rule (later vacated by the 8th Circuit, now being revived). (https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/negative-option-rule)
- **Coaching feels scripted / bot-like.** "Human coach access is limited and often feels scripted, not personalized... many users report that coach responses feel templated." Quality "varies greatly depending on your assigned coach." Compounding this: "Noom is reportedly laying off up to a quarter of its wellness coaches" and shifting to scheduled video calls. (https://askvora.com/alternatives/noom; https://www.aol.com/news/noom-reported-layoffs-101346556.html)
- **Repetitive lessons.** "Some users found the daily lessons too repetitive after a while." "After the initial weeks, the daily content sometimes circles back to concepts already covered." (https://orionhealthwellness.com/noom-diet-review/; https://nutrola.app/en/blog/noom-vs-myfitnesspal-for-beginners-2026)
- **Expensive.** "$60–70/month" cited repeatedly as the core resentment point vs. free MyFitnessPal. (https://nutrola.app/en/blog/why-is-noom-so-expensive)
- **Mediocre food database / logging friction.** "Noom's food database is mediocre, with a smaller database, frustrating search, and less reliable barcode scanning" — a key reason people quit the actual tracking. (https://nutrola.app/en/blog/noom-vs-myfitnesspal-for-beginners-2026)
- **Trustpilot caveat.** Headline rating is high (~4.4) "with mostly five-star reviews," but reviewers flag "multiple five-star reviews appearing back-to-back on the same day" and "copy-paste scripts" in Noom's responses — suggesting review-management rather than organic praise. (https://www.nutritionnc.com/glp-1/reviews/noom/) Contrast with PissedConsumer (~2.4K reviews, heavily negative) and an active BBB complaint file. (https://noom.pissedconsumer.com/review.html)

### Direct quotes (Noom)
- "CHARGED MY ACCOUNT $160 WITHOUT INDICATION. I tried to cancel my account the day after I installed the app. Two weeks later I received a charge of $161.37." — ConsumerAffairs (https://www.consumeraffairs.com/health/noom.html)
- "I cancelled a subscription with noom and did not receive my refund... I was then charged an additional $854.00 again on April 23 2026." — ConsumerAffairs (https://www.consumeraffairs.com/health/noom.html)
- "[Suddenly charged] for an auto-subscription, $126" a year after cancellation; told repeatedly they "had not canceled and were not eligible for a refund." — ConsumerAffairs (https://www.consumeraffairs.com/news/noom-auto-renewal-charges-lead-to-spike-in-consumer-complaints-082120.html)

### Noom feature requests / quit reasons
- Less repetitive, more advanced lesson progression after week 2–3.
- Genuinely personalized (not templated) coaching; transparency that some support is automated.
- A better food database + faster barcode scanning (logging is the weak link).
- Clear renewal notices and one-click cancellation (the settlement-mandated changes are exactly what users demand).

## LIFESUM

Liked for polished design and structured meal plans; 2025–2026 sentiment is dominated by backlash against a forced AI redesign plus steady billing complaints.

### What users LOVE
- **Clean, simple, attractive UI.** Long-term fans "praise the app's design and simplicity"; one 4-year user gave 4/5 stars highlighting "the barcode scanner, progress charts, and curated meal plans as standout features." (https://nutriscan.app/blog/posts/lifesum-premium-worth-it-2026-meal-plans-macros-cost-6ffc879a6c)
- **Themed meal plans / guided eating.** "Visually appealing meal plans and themed diets (keto, Mediterranean, high protein)" — appeals to users who "want guided eating rather than pure tracking." Plus "integrated fasting timers alongside calorie tracking." (https://nutrola.app/en/blog/best-yazio-alternatives-2026)

### What users HATE
- **The Feb 2025 AI tracker redesign — biggest current grievance.** "Cashews identified as shrimp, coffee added to logs from background mug photos, barcode scans that double or triple calories, forced logouts on app reopen, and Apple Health sync failures after updates." "The new flat AI input replaced the structured per-meal flow that long-term users relied on." Some Trustpilot reviewers say "after paying for Premium, the app launched an AI-driven interface that calculated values incorrectly." (https://nutriscan.app/blog/posts/lifesum-premium-worth-it-2026-meal-plans-macros-cost-6ffc879a6c; https://www.trustpilot.com/review/lifesum.com)
- **Billing / subscription traps.** "$2/month offer that charged the whole year without clear notification." "Auto-renewal disputes where users believed they had cancelled, and PayPal subscriptions that continued to charge past in-app cancellation." Critical UX trap: **"Deleting the Lifesum app does NOT cancel your subscription"** — you must cancel via the OS store. (https://justuseapp.com/en/app/286906691/lifesum-diet-macro-tracker/reviews; https://nutriscan.app/blog/posts/lifesum-premium-worth-it-2026-meal-plans-macros-cost-6ffc879a6c)
- **Inconsistent pricing.** "Frustration comes from inconsistent pricing (different users see different annual costs)." Premium runs "$3.75–$10/month in 2026." (https://nutriscan.app/blog/posts/lifesum-premium-worth-it-2026-meal-plans-macros-cost-6ffc879a6c)
- **Heavy paywall / thin free tier.** "Many advanced features and personalized plans are only available through a paid subscription, limiting free users' experience." It's specifically flagged as "too expensive" relative to alternatives. (https://nutrola.app/en/blog/lifesum-is-too-expensive-what-else-can-i-use)
- **Post-update reliability bugs / logging friction.** "Updates don't work properly and manual log features don't save"; "in-app support chat crashing repeatedly." (search-snippet, https://justuseapp.com/en/app/286906691/lifesum-diet-macro-tracker/reviews)

### Lifesum feature requests / quit reasons
- Restore the old structured per-meal logging flow; make AI tracking optional, not forced.
- Fix AI accuracy (mis-IDs, double-counted barcodes) and Apple Health sync.
- Consistent/transparent pricing and clearer renewal disclosure; honor in-app cancellation.
- Reduce paywall aggressiveness on core tracking.

## YAZIO

The "budget favorite" — praised for polished design, fasting tools, and low price; main gripes are ads, creeping paywall (features that used to be free), and a Euro-centric food database.

### What users LOVE
- **Polished, smooth design.** "One of the most polished tracker interfaces available." "YAZIO feels smoother and more enjoyable" than MyFitnessPal; "wins on design, price, barcode accessibility, and fasting tools." Rated ~4.6 on ~300K+ reviews. (https://yourappland.com/yazio-vs-myfitnesspal-which-nutritio-app-is-better/; https://nutrola.app/en/blog/best-yazio-alternatives-2026)
- **Intermittent fasting tracker** — the standout differentiator. "If fasting tracking is your priority, YAZIO appears to be the better option with dedicated fasting features." (https://yourappland.com/yazio-vs-myfitnesspal-which-nutritio-app-is-better/)
- **Affordability.** PRO ~$3.99/mo effective on the annual plan (~$38–48/yr), cheaper than MyFitnessPal Premium ($79.99/yr); 7-day free trial. (https://nutriscan.app/blog/posts/yazio-pro-worth-it-2026-4f450da831)

### What users HATE
- **Ads and relentless upgrade pop-ups in free tier.** "Banner ads and frequent pop-ups encouraging upgrade." "Upgrade prompts after nearly every action." "Persistent pop-ups... which can interrupt the logging experience." (https://nutrola.app/en/blog/best-yazio-alternatives-2026)
- **Creeping paywall — features that used to be free.** Verbatim: **"I still love YAZIO, but it used to not have ads after each track, and you used to be able to use barcode scanning without premium."** Meal plans, detailed nutrients, and fasting analytics are PRO-gated; AI photo scanner is PRO-only. (https://nutrola.app/en/blog/best-yazio-alternatives-2026; https://nutriscan.app/blog/posts/yazio-pricing-2026-free-vs-pro-what-pro-unlocks-33b26f8fc7)
- **Euro-centric food database gaps.** "Covers European products well, [but] users in North America, Asia, and other regions often find gaps"; non-European entries are user-submitted, raising accuracy concerns. (https://nutrola.app/en/blog/best-yazio-alternatives-2026)
- **Logging friction.** Barcode scanner is "relatively slower at around 2–3 seconds per scan"; AI photo scanner "common foods work well; mixed dishes and regional foods often need manual edits." (https://nutriscan.app/blog/posts/yazio-pro-worth-it-2026-4f450da831)
- **Custom-food limitation.** "Homemade food created without a barcode is automatically private and cannot be made public." (https://help.yazio.com/hc/en-us/articles/360000629817)

### YAZIO feature requests / quit reasons
- Remove (or reduce) the post-log ad and bring back free barcode scanning — the #1 resentment is regression of previously-free features.
- Expand/clean the North American + Asian food database.
- Faster barcode scanning; better AI handling of mixed/regional dishes.
- More fasting programs and recipes in the free tier.

## Cross-app synthesis (themes for a competitor)

1. **Billing/subscription dark patterns are the single biggest brand killer** — catastrophic for Noom (class action + FTC-era scrutiny), moderate but real for Lifesum (auto-renew/PayPal disputes, "deleting ≠ cancelling") and YAZIO (creeping paywall on once-free features). A transparent, one-click-cancel, clearly-disclosed-renewal model is a strong differentiator.
2. **Logging friction is why people quit the actual tracking** across all three: Noom's weak food DB + slow barcode; YAZIO's slow scan + regional gaps; Lifesum's broken/forced AI input. Database quality + fast, reliable logging is table stakes.
3. **"Free feature regression" and forced redesigns breed resentment** — YAZIO moving barcode behind paywall, Lifesum forcing AI over the trusted structured flow. Don't take away what users already had.
4. **Behavior-change content is genuinely valued** (Noom's psychology lessons) **but must not be repetitive**, and "human coaching" that's actually scripted/automated is worse than honestly automated.
5. **Pricing transparency beats low price** — Lifesum's inconsistent pricing and Noom's "8 months upfront" surprise generate more anger than the dollar amount itself.
